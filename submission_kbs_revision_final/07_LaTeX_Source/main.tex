\begin{abstract}
Caching with predictions is useful only when those predictions change the eviction that is actually made. We study unweighted online paging as candidate-level scoring: at each full-cache miss, \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} estimates a finite-horizon replay loss for every resident item under a fixed continuation rule and evicts the apparent least-harmful candidate. Under a matched protocol against LRU, SIEVE, FIFO-Reinsertion, LRB, 3L-Cache, CACHEUS, and HALP, the method loses on a clear majority of 21 cells against every baseline. A full-pipeline objective ablation likewise finds eviction-loss worst or tied-worst, but a matched common-model control that changes only the training objective does \emph{not} support blaming that label: eviction-loss is nominally best by total misses. The learned scorer agrees with the exact target on $97.5\%$ of decisions, yet every measured non-LRU exact-oracle decision has multiple optimal actions (mean optimal-set fraction $\approx 0.991$). A deterministic exact oracle loses to LRU on 18 of 21 cells ($+81{,}750$ misses), but choosing the LRU-most minimizer never loses ($-413$ misses); the deficit is a tie-breaking confound, not proof that the exact target intrinsically loses to LRU. Continuation-label correction helps in most but not all cells; a generic DAgger-style shift correction does not. Wall-clock cost is orders of magnitude above lightweight baselines. The leading supported limitation is action-underdetermined supervision, not a deployment-ready replacement policy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Problem and Research Question}
\label{subsec:problem_motivation}
\label{subsec:objective_scope}

In online paging, a full-cache miss requires choosing which resident item to evict without future requests \cite{belady1966replacement_algorithms,fiat1991competitive_paging}. Learning-augmented caching shows that predictions can help when they are informative and used carefully, but gains are regime-dependent and vanish when advice is noisy or weakly tied to the replacement objective \cite{lykouris2021competitive_caching,antoniadis2020online_metric_untrusted,im2022parsimonious_lac,wei2020better_simpler_lac,chledowski2021robust_lac_experimental,mitzenmacher2022algorithms_predictions}. The operational question is therefore candidate selection: which currently cached item is safest to remove, not merely whether a hint should be trusted.

This paper tests a seemingly natural answer---supervise each candidate by the finite-horizon downstream miss cost of evicting it---and asks whether \emph{decision alignment} is sufficient for useful online control. We distinguish three properties and evaluate them separately: alignment (the label is the eviction cost, not an indirect proxy); \emph{decision informativeness} (the label actually separates candidates); and \emph{deployment efficiency} (online scoring cost is acceptable). Alignment is built in by construction; the other two are empirical. Scope is unweighted paging with unit miss cost. We claim neither a competitive-ratio guarantee nor a deployment-ready replacement policy.

\subsection{Main Contributions}
\label{subsec:main_contributions}

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate paging as candidate-level scoring and instantiate a finite-horizon eviction-value predictor, \texttt{evict\_value\_v1}.
    \item Under a matched protocol (identical hashed traces, capacities, windows, and miss accounting) against seven classical and learned baselines, including LRB and 3L-Cache, \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} does not outperform any baseline.
    \item A full-pipeline ablation against reuse-distance, next-arrival, and pairwise-preference supervision finds eviction-loss worst or tied-worst. A matched common-model control that changes only the training objective does \emph{not} support blaming that objective: eviction-loss is nominally best by aggregate misses.
    \item Mechanistic diagnostics (exact oracle, degeneracy audit, learned/exact agreement, and tie-aware replay) disfavor gross fitting failure and insufficient data. The horizon-4 target is highly action-underdetermined; the deterministic exact-oracle deficit versus LRU is a tie-breaking confound. Continuation-label mismatch is a partial, family-dependent contributor; generic DAgger-style shift correction does not improve online misses.
    \item Controlled timing shows per-decision cost orders of magnitude above lightweight baselines. Combined with the negative miss-ratio result, the present implementation is neither performance-competitive nor deployment-efficient.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related Work}
\label{subsec:related_work}

Belady's offline rule and competitive paging frame the core difficulty: eviction quality depends on future reuse, yet the choice is online \cite{belady1966replacement_algorithms,fiat1991competitive_paging}. Learning-augmented paging typically uses next-arrival or action advice with robustness when advice is wrong \cite{lykouris2021competitive_caching,rohatgi2020near_optimal_caching,bansal2022learning_augmented_weighted_paging,im2022parsimonious_lac,antoniadis2020online_metric_untrusted,wei2020better_simpler_lac,chledowski2021robust_lac_experimental,blum2000online_learning_mts}. We share the robustness concern but study a candidate-level cost target rather than hint-trust or expert switching.

Learned replacement systems supervise from future-derived labels: PARROT imitates Belady; LRB and Raven predict Belady-guided reuse; Mockingjay estimates reuse times; HALP learns candidate preferences; 3L-Cache combines a lightweight scorer with bidirectional sampling; CACHEUS mixes eviction experts \cite{liu2020parrot,song2020lrb,hu2022raven,shah2022mockingjay,song2023halp,zhou2025threelcache,rodriguez2021cacheus}. LRB, 3L-Cache, CACHEUS, and HALP are compared directly in Section~\ref{subsec:major1_comparison}. Proxies in this literature (oracle actions, reuse distance, next arrival, pairwise order) still require translation into an eviction. Our target is a counterfactual finite-horizon miss count. Structural alignment does not imply informativeness; we use decision-focused/predict-then-optimize language only as a conceptual link, including the observation that multiple optima are a form of degeneracy \cite{elmachtoub2022smart_predict_optimize,wilder2019decision_focused_learning,shah2022lodl,elbalghiti2022generalization}.

MUSTACHE forecasts pages over a horizon; we supervise eviction cost instead \cite{tolomei2022mustache}. MAT, GL-Cache, LAH/S4-FIFO, and Merlin reduce hot-path inference; S3-FIFO and SIEVE show that simple queues can be competitive \cite{yang2023mat,yang2023glcache,xia2026lah,li2026merlin,yang2023s3fifo,zhang2024sieve}. Our comparison includes SIEVE and FIFO-Reinsertion as lightweight anchors.

\section{Main Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System Model and Preliminaries}
\label{subsec:system_model}

We study unweighted paging. Let $\sigma=(r_1,\ldots,r_T)$ with $r_t\in\mathcal{P}$. The cache has capacity $k$; $C_t\subseteq\mathcal{P}$ is the content before $r_t$. A miss with $|C_t|=k$ requires evicting one resident item \cite{belady1966replacement_algorithms,fiat1991competitive_paging}. Each miss has unit cost, so
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cost}(\sigma)=\sum_{t=1}^{T} m_t.
\label{eq:total-cost}
\end{equation}
The eviction candidates are $\mathcal{V}_t=C_t$. For candidate $q\in\mathcal{V}_t$ and horizon $H\geq 1$,
\begin{equation}
L_H(q,t)=\sum_{\tau=t+1}^{t+H} m_{\tau}^{(q,t)}
\label{eq:eviction_loss}
\end{equation}
is the miss count over the next $H$ requests after evicting $q$ at $t$ and replaying under a fixed continuation rule (here, lightweight LRU-style replay). This is a local proxy, not an offline optimum. We keep a single definition of $L_H$; the learned policy approximates it.

\subsection{Eviction-Value Prediction Framework}
\label{subsec:eviction_value_framework}

Each candidate receives a feature vector $x(q,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ summarizing the request, cache state, and candidate context. Features fall into six groups: request-side context, candidate recency/reuse, predictor--LRU comparison, cache-state summary, predictor/recency disagreement, and recent activity. The repository holds the full specification. The scorer is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{L}_H(q,t)=f_{\theta}\!\bigl(x(q,t)\bigr),
\label{eq:predicted_loss}
\end{equation}
and the victim is
\begin{equation}
q_t^{*}=\arg\min_{q\in\mathcal{V}_t}\widehat{L}_H(q,t).
\label{eq:eviction_rule}
\end{equation}
An optional early-return guard that can temporarily delegate to a conservative baseline exists in the repository; it is unvalidated and unused in any quantitative claim (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).

\subsection{Algorithmic Workflow}
\label{subsec:workflow_implementation}

Figure~\ref{fig:method_overview} summarizes the pipeline. Traces are converted to a common format. At each full-cache miss, one supervised example per candidate is built from $x(q,t)$ and $L_H(q,t)$. A scorer is fit; online, all $k$ residents are scored and the $\arg\min$ of \eqref{eq:eviction_rule} is evicted. Learned policies and baselines share the same simulator.

\label{subsec:experimental_setup}

All policies run in one simulator on the same processed traces, capacities, request order, and loader. The study has two parts: offline scorer selection on $L_H$, then online replay of the induced policy. Offline target quality need not imply online miss-ratio gains. The setting is unweighted paging; the primary metric is total misses. Claims are restricted to the reported artifacts.

\subsection{Matched Comparison Against Learned Cache-Replacement Baselines}
\label{subsec:major1_comparison}

This section is the primary evidence: leave-one-family-out training of \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} and a matched comparison to LRB, 3L-Cache, CACHEUS, HALP, LRU, SIEVE, and FIFO-Reinsertion. It also removes the train/test overlap of the historical model behind Table~\ref{tab:family-capacity-gap}.

\subsubsection{Corrected training protocol}

\texttt{evict\_value\_v1} is retrained under leave-one-family-out: for each of seven families, the model is trained on the other six (five train, one validation-only), and the held-out family contributes no rows to training, validation, hyperparameter, feature, horizon, seed, or early-stopping choices. This removes the overlap behind Table~\ref{tab:family-capacity-gap}.

\subsubsection{Matched evaluation protocol}

All eight policies use identical traces (SHA-256), capacities $\{32,64,128\}$, history prefix $[0,10{,}000)$, scored suffix $[10{,}000,50{,}000)$, unit-object slots, and hit/miss accounting. Matching is evaluation-side only: LRB, 3L-Cache, and CACHEUS adapt online on the scored stream; HALP trains on each trace's own prefix; LRU/SIEVE/FIFO-Reinsertion are parameter-free; \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} is leave-one-family-out. No policy sees future requests or another family's data.

\subsection{Mechanistic Diagnosis of the Eviction-Loss Target}
\label{subsec:mechanistic_diagnosis}

The matched comparison and pipeline ablation are negative; Table~\ref{tab:common-model-v2} shows the training objective is not the isolated cause. We diagnose the target on the same 21 cells.

\textbf{Is inaccurate prediction the cause?} An exact horizon-4 solver for the same target agrees with the learned scorer on $97.53\%$ of decisions (macro mean set-aware agreement), with mean target regret $0.0247$ and positive-regret fraction $0.0247$. Learned misses ($601{,}569$) are within $6.4\%$ of LRU ($565{,}126$). Gross fitting failure is disfavored.

\textbf{Is the target useful if optimized perfectly?} Under deterministic tie-breaking (minimum candidate identifier), the exact oracle has mean miss ratio $0.770090$ vs.\ $0.672769$ for LRU: 0 wins / 3 ties / 18 losses, $+81{,}750$ misses ($646{,}876$ vs.\ $565{,}126$). That instantiation is a poor online policy; it is not a statement about every way of resolving the same optimal sets.

A tie-aware control varies only how exact minimizers are chosen. Every measured non-LRU oracle decision has multiple optima (\texttt{fraction\_tied\_decisions}${}=1.0$ on all $168$ non-LRU rows; mean fraction all-tied $\approx 0.649$; mean optimal-set fraction $\approx 0.991$; Wikimedia fully degenerate). LRU-within-minima never loses to LRU (16/5/0; $-413$ misses; $564{,}713$ vs.\ $565{,}126$). MRU-within-minima matches the deterministic deficit (0/3/18; $+89{,}135$). Uniform random among minimizers (five seeds) usually loses to LRU (7/15/83 over $105$ rows) but usually beats the deterministic oracle (90/15/0). The deterministic deficit is therefore a tie-breaking confound. It does \emph{not} show that the exact H4 target intrinsically loses to LRU. Downstream selection among minimizers can move the result from a large deficit to a small LRU advantage.

\textbf{Why so underdetermined?} Independently of tie rules: unique-winner fraction $0.0$; multiple-optimum fraction $1.0$; $76.4\%$ of strict-preference decisions are all-tied; mean optimal-set fraction $0.9949$. Across $41{,}615{,}776$ candidate-residency observations at $565{,}126$ decisions, $P(T>4)=0.9939$ overall and $0.9793$ even conditioned on eventual reuse. Most consequences lie outside $H=4$, so the target often cannot distinguish candidates.

\textbf{Summary.} A learning-curve check at $1$--$50\%$ of candidate examples shows no material target-quality gain (H1 disfavored). Fitting failure is disfavored; the eviction-loss \emph{objective} is not supported as the culprit (Table~\ref{tab:common-model-v2}). The strongest supported diagnosis is action-underdetermined $H=4$ supervision. Tie degeneracy is not claimed to explain every aspect of learned underperformance. Section~\ref{subsec:continuation_ablations} tests a secondary continuation/deployment contributor.

\subsection{Continuation-Mismatch and Distribution-Shift Ablations}
\label{subsec:continuation_ablations}

Two controlled tests address continuation labels vs.\ generic state shift, previously speculative.

\emph{C0} is LRU. \emph{C1} trains $\pi_1$ on LRU-continuation labels (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eviction_loss}) and deploys $\pi_1$ recursively. \emph{C2} retrains $\pi_2$ on frozen-$\pi_1$ continuation and deploys $\pi_2$; that is the only C1$\to$C2 change. C2 improves on 13 cells, ties 3 (Wikimedia), worsens 5 (macro mean $-0.0102$; $601{,}569\to 592{,}970$ misses vs.\ C0 $565{,}126$). BrightKite at capacity 32 regresses $+0.2433$. Continuation mismatch is \emph{partially supported and regime-dependent}, not a full explanation.

A one-step DAgger-style mix of on- and off-policy states reduces a generic shift index in 16 of 21 cells, yet misses improve in 2, tie in 3, and worsen in 16 (macro $+0.0094$; $591{,}604\to 599{,}537$). In 13 of 18 informative cells the shift metric improves while misses worsen. The tested generic correction is a negative result; it does not imply that distribution shift is absent.

\subsection{Discussion and Synthesis}
\label{subsec:discussion_analysis}
\label{subsec:implications}

The canonical interpretation is as follows. Alignment of $L_H$ with the eviction action does not imply informativeness or efficiency. Offline, short-horizon nonlinear scorers learn the target (Section~\ref{subsec:main_results}). Online, \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} loses to all seven matched baselines (Section~\ref{subsec:major1_comparison}). In the deployed pipeline eviction-loss is worst of four objectives (Table~\ref{tab:objective-comparison}), but matched Common-Model V2 does not support blaming that training objective (Table~\ref{tab:common-model-v2}). H3 target/tie degeneracy is strongly supported: every measured non-LRU exact decision has multiple optima, and deterministic exact-oracle failure vs.\ LRU is tie-confounded (Section~\ref{subsec:mechanistic_diagnosis}). Exact-target intrinsic inferiority is not supported. Continuation mismatch is a partial, family-dependent contributor; the tested DAgger correction does not help (Section~\ref{subsec:continuation_ablations}). The current implementation is neither miss-competitive nor deployment-efficient.

\subsection{Overhead and Scalability}
\label{subsec:overhead_scalability}

Offline label construction required about 10.3 hours and 96\,GB (662 shards); fitting all horizon/model configurations took about 7--8 minutes. Online, each miss scores all $k$ residents ($O(k)$ inferences) versus $O(1)$ for LRU, SIEVE, and FIFO-Reinsertion. \subsection{Practical Significance}
\label{subsec:practical_significance}

\texttt{evict\_value\_v1} is not a replacement for LRU, SIEVE, FIFO-Reinsertion, or the learned baselines in latency-sensitive deployments; no validated deployment scenario is claimed. The contribution is diagnostic: a controlled test of a finite-horizon candidate-level target, showing that informativeness and cost---not merely alignment---must hold. Future practical redesign would need a more informative target and cheaper inference \cite{yang2023mat,yang2023glcache,xia2026lah,zhou2025threelcache,li2026merlin,yang2023s3fifo,zhang2024sieve}.

\section{Conclusions and Future Research}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of Findings}
\label{subsec:summary_findings}

Candidate-level $H=4$ eviction-value scoring is learnable offline and unsuccessful online. See Section~\ref{subsec:discussion_analysis} for the mechanistic synthesis; we do not repeat it here. Decision alignment without informativeness and efficiency is insufficient. The negative result is for this target, horizon, and pipeline, not for all candidate-level learning.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Unweighted unit-size paging only.
\item Finite $H=4$ local proxy, not an offline-optimal rule.
\item Implementation fidelity: 3L-Cache untuned batch size; CACHEUS provenance; HALP low-to-medium reimplementation (Section~\ref{subsec:major1_comparison}).
\item 21 cells, fixed seeds, descriptive paired results; BrightKite/Citi Bike are event streams; Wikimedia is a pageview proxy; 50k-request windows.
\item Historical family gaps (Table~\ref{tab:family-capacity-gap}) have train/test overlap; primary evidence is Section~\ref{subsec:major1_comparison}. Capacity 256 unevaluated.
\item \texttt{evict\_value\_v1} timing is a single run, not in the 5-repetition campaign.
\item No validated fallback; repository guard is unused for claims.
\item Single-author revision; audits do not replace independent replication.
\item Diagnostics constrain interpretation; they do not isolate every downstream cause of underperformance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Research Directions}
\label{subsec:future_research}

Priorities are informative, tie-aware targets; adaptive/longer horizons or terminal correction; iterative continuation correction; selective inference; weighted/variable-size caching; and a validated fallback. Ranking supervision remains open, but not because eviction-loss is a uniquely harmful matched objective. Capacity-256 sweeps remain undone.
